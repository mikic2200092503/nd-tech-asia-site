The Material

What this resin is, and what it isn't

Ion exchange resin is a bead-form material that swaps dissolved ions in water for others of the same charge — used to soften water, remove dissolved salts, or strip out specific contaminants. Mixed-bed resin combines cation and anion beads in one vessel for high-purity "polishing" duty, most often in semiconductor, power and pharmaceutical water systems.

Both lots listed here were originally manufactured for exactly that kind of high-purity polishing service. They have since been used and removed from their original system — we are not a resin manufacturer or regenerator, and this stock has not been chemically regenerated or reprocessed by us.

We're simply passing on surplus material that would otherwise sit unused. Everything below is described as accurately as we know it — where we're not certain of a figure, we say so and invite you to test a sample instead of guessing.

Where This Type Is Used

What mixed-bed ion exchange resin is normally used for

For context if you're new to this material — mixed-bed resin like this is built for high-purity polishing, not basic water softening. The right application depends on the purity level you actually need.

Semiconductor ultrapure water

Final polishing step to remove trace ions, silica and TOC before water contacts wafers or components.

Power generation

Boiler and steam-turbine feedwater demineralization, protecting equipment from scale and corrosion.

Pharmaceutical & lab water

High-purity water for pharmaceutical processing and laboratory-grade applications requiring near-total ion removal.

Demineralization / desalting

Removing nearly all dissolved salts from water — the core job a mixed bed does, regardless of industry.

General industrial polishing

Final ion polishing after reverse osmosis, where standard filtration alone can't reach the purity required.
